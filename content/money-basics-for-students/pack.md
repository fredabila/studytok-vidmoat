## About
Nobody is taught how money works, and then everybody is expected to manage it. These notes cover
the handful of ideas that explain most of it: where your money goes, what interest really is and
why it is wonderful on savings and brutal on debt, how inflation quietly changes prices, and what
"risk" means in plain terms. Everything here is general knowledge for understanding your own
situation, not advice on what to buy.

## Units
### Where the money goes
A budget is just a list of what comes in and what goes out, sorted into needs, wants and savings.
The point is not to stop spending; it is to see the pattern. Most people underestimate small
repeated costs: a snack a day is a textbook a term. One useful rule of thumb is 50/30/20: about
half for needs, under a third for wants, and a fifth saved or used to clear debt. The numbers are
less important than the habit of looking.
- The 50/30/20 guideline was popularised by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in a 2005 book.
- Fixed costs (rent, fees, transport passes) are the same every month; variable costs (food, data, going out) change and are easier to adjust.

### Interest: money that earns money
Interest is the price of using someone else's money. Save 100 at 5% a year and you have 105 after a
year, 110.25 after two, because the second year's interest is earned on the first year's too. That
is compounding, and over long periods it dominates: at 7% a year, money doubles roughly every ten
years. The rule of 72 estimates the doubling time: divide 72 by the rate. Starting early matters
more than starting big.
- Simple interest is paid on the original amount only; compound interest is paid on the amount plus previous interest.
- Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual rate in percent (72 ÷ 6 = about 12 years at 6%).

### Debt: the same engine in reverse
Borrowing runs compounding against you. A loan or credit balance at 24% a year costs about 2% a
month, and if you pay only the minimum the balance barely moves. Two numbers tell you what a loan
really costs: the annual percentage rate (APR), and the total you will have repaid by the end.
Mobile-money and payday-style loans often quote small weekly fees that work out to very high
annual rates. Debt is not always bad (a loan that pays for a qualification can pay for itself),
but you should always know the rate.
- APR is the yearly cost of borrowing including fees, expressed as a percentage.
- A 2% monthly rate compounds to about 26.8% a year, not 24%.

### Inflation: prices drift
If prices rise 10% in a year, the same money buys 10% less; your savings lost value even though
the number did not change. Inflation is measured by tracking the price of a fixed basket of goods
(the consumer price index). It is why wages, pensions and savings rates are compared with
inflation: a 5% savings rate during 10% inflation is a real loss of about 5%. Central banks raise
interest rates to slow inflation and cut them to encourage spending.
- The real interest rate is roughly the nominal rate minus the inflation rate.
- Ghana's inflation peaked above 50% in early 2023 and fell sharply through 2024, a vivid recent example of why the real rate matters.

### Risk and the long run
Higher expected returns come with more ups and downs; there is no safe 20% a year, and anyone
promising one is a warning sign. Savings accounts are stable and low-return; shares of companies
have historically grown more over decades but can fall by half in a bad year. Spreading money
across many investments (diversification) reduces the damage any one can do. The single most
reliable protection is time: the longer you can leave money alone, the less a bad year matters.
- Diversification means holding many different investments so no single failure wipes you out.
- Historically, broad stock markets have returned more than cash over periods of 20 years or longer, with large swings in between.

### Scams and small print
Pressure to act now, guaranteed returns, requests to pay a fee to receive money, and messages
from "your bank" asking for a PIN are the four signatures of fraud. Legitimate institutions never
ask for your PIN or full password. For any contract, read the fee section and the exit terms
before the headline rate. If an offer only makes sense if you recruit others, it is a pyramid.
- A pyramid or Ponzi scheme pays earlier participants with later participants' money and collapses when recruitment slows.
- Never share a one-time password (OTP) sent to your phone; it is the key to your account.

## Facts
- Compounding means interest earns interest; the effect grows faster the longer the period.
- A consumer price index tracks the cost of a fixed basket of goods and services over time.
- Diversification lowers risk without necessarily lowering expected return.
- An emergency fund of three to six months of essential costs is a common guideline for financial stability.
