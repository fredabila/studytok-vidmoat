## About
For two centuries the Asante (Ashanti) state was the most powerful in what is now Ghana: a
confederation founded around 1700 at Kumasi, held together by the Golden Stool, grown rich on
gold and trade, organised through an unusually capable bureaucracy, and able to fight the British
Empire to a standstill more than once. These notes cover its founding, how it was governed, its
economy, the wars with Britain, and what remains of it today. Pitched at West African senior high
school history.

## Units
### Osei Tutu and the Golden Stool
In the late 1600s the Akan state of Kwaman, under Osei Tutu, broke free of the overlordship of
Denkyira. With his adviser, the priest Okomfo Anokye, he united the surrounding Akan states into a
confederation. Tradition says Anokye commanded the Golden Stool (Sika Dwa Kofi) down from the sky
to rest on Osei Tutu's knees: it embodies the soul of the nation and no one, not even the king,
sits on it. The confederation defeated Denkyira at the battle of Feyiase around 1701, the usual
date for the empire's founding.
- Osei Tutu was the first Asantehene (king of Asante); Kumasi became the capital.
- The Golden Stool is never sat upon and is kept out of public view; it symbolises the unity of the Asante people.

### How the empire was run
The Asantehene ruled from Kumasi with a council of chiefs and a growing class of appointed
officials, including treasury, trade and diplomatic posts, which made the state more
bureaucratic than most of its neighbours. Conquered states kept their own chiefs but paid tribute
and sent soldiers. A network of roads radiated from Kumasi, and the Asante used a sophisticated
system of weights for gold dust as currency. Under Osei Kwadwo (ruled 1764 to 1777) promotion by
merit, not only by birth, became the norm for many offices, a reform historians call the
"Kwadwoan revolution".
- Gold dust was the currency; brass goldweights (abrammuo) in the shape of proverbs and animals measured it.
- Great roads, the nkwantempon, linked Kumasi to the provinces and the coast.

### Gold, kola and the trade routes
Asante wealth came from gold mines, kola nuts sold north to the Sahel, and control of trade
between the forest and the coast. Northward routes connected to trans-Saharan trade; southward
routes ran to the European forts on the Gold Coast, where gold, ivory and, in the 1700s and early
1800s, enslaved people were exchanged for guns, cloth and metal. Control of these routes was the
main reason for Asante expansion toward the coast and the main source of conflict with the Fante
states and the British.
- Kola nuts, a mild stimulant, were a major export to Muslim markets in the north.
- Firearms obtained through coastal trade helped Asante armies dominate their neighbours.

### The Anglo-Asante wars
Asante and Britain fought five wars between 1824 and 1900. In the first, Asante forces killed the
British governor Sir Charles MacCarthy at Nsamankow (1824). In 1874 a British expedition under
Garnet Wolseley burned Kumasi. In 1896 the Asantehene Prempeh I was deposed and exiled to the
Seychelles without a fight. In 1900 the governor Frederick Hodgson demanded to sit on the Golden
Stool; the queen mother of Ejisu, Yaa Asantewaa, led the war that followed, which ended with
Asante annexed as a British colony in 1902.
- The 1900 conflict is called the War of the Golden Stool or the Yaa Asantewaa War.
- Prempeh I returned from exile in 1924 and was recognised as Kumasihene in 1926; the Asante Confederacy was formally restored in 1935.

### Asante today
The Asante Kingdom continues inside the Republic of Ghana as a traditional state. The Asantehene,
Otumfuo Osei Tutu II since 1999, presides from the Manhyia Palace in Kumasi and is one of the
most influential traditional rulers in Africa. Kente cloth, Adinkra symbols, the Akwasidae
festival and the Golden Stool remain central to Asante identity, and Kumasi is Ghana's second
largest city.
- Manhyia Palace houses a museum on the history of the kings.
- Akwasidae is celebrated every six weeks on the Asante calendar, when the Asantehene sits in state.

## Facts
- The Asante Empire was founded around 1701 after the defeat of Denkyira at Feyiase.
- Its capital, Kumasi, lies in the forest zone of central Ghana.
- Twi, an Akan language, is the language of the Asante.
- The empire at its height in the early 1800s covered most of present-day Ghana and parts of neighbouring Côte d'Ivoire and Togo.
- Yaa Asantewaa died in exile in the Seychelles in 1921.
